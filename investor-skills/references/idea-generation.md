# Idea Generation

Screens surface **candidates, not conclusions** — every screen hit still needs the full research snapshot (stock-research.md) and often a valuation pass before it's an idea.

## Step 1 — Frame the search

Ask (or infer from the user's portfolio): direction (long only for most personal investors), market cap, sector, style (value / growth / quality / special situation), geography (US, VN, global), and any theme. Two or three constraints beat ten.

## Step 2 — Run a screen

Pick the template matching the style. Numbers are starting filters — loosen them if the universe is empty, tighten if it returns 100 names:

**Value:** P/E below sector median · EV/EBITDA below own 5Y average · FCF yield >5% · insider buying in last 90 days (US) or major-holder accumulation (VN disclosure)

**Growth:** revenue growth >15% YoY · earnings growth >20% · growth accelerating · margins expanding · ROIC >15%

**Quality:** 5+ years consistent revenue growth · stable/expanding margins · ROE >15% · low debt/equity · high FCF conversion · insider/founder ownership meaningful

**Short / avoid (usually used to screen holdings to trim):** decelerating growth · margin compression · receivables or inventory outgrowing sales · valuation premium to peers without a driver · accounting flags (auditor change, restatement, frequent one-offs)

**Special situation:** spin-offs <12M old · post-restructuring · activist involvement · management change at an underperformer · lockup expiry overhang

Where to screen: the agent runs these qualitatively via web search on sector lists the user provides or well-known indexes (S&P 500, VN30); if the user has a screener (VNDirect, cafef, TradingView, finviz), generate the filter setup for them to paste.

## Step 3 — Thematic sweep (if theme-based)

1. Write the theme as a testable statement ("AI capex stays elevated through 2027").
2. Map the value chain: direct beneficiaries → suppliers → second-order names the market hasn't connected yet.
3. Separate pure-play vs diversified exposure (diversified = diluted but safer).
4. Judge what's already priced in vs under-appreciated — a right theme with everything already expensive is not an idea.

## Step 4 — Present the shortlist

5-10 names max, each as:

```
[Company] — Long — one-line thesis
| Metric | Value | vs peers |
(market cap, EV/EBITDA or P/E, growth, margin, FCF yield)
Why mispriced: ...
What market misses: ...
Catalyst: ...
Killer risk: ...
```

Plus one comparison table across all names and a priority order (which to research first: biggest mispricing × nearest catalyst).

## Notes

- The best ideas live at intersections: quality business at a value price because of a temporary, fixable headwind.
- Contrarian ideas need a catalyst — early without a catalyst is just wrong for a long time.
- Check crowding before sizing: heavy analyst coverage + crowded positioning = slow, contested upside.
- Track the hit rate of past screens; kill the filters that keep producing losers.
- For a personal book, an "avoid/trim" screen on current holdings is often worth more than a new-ideas screen.

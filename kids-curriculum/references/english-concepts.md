# English Language Arts Concepts — Grades 3–5 Scope & Sequence

ELA has four strands: **Reading, Writing, Language (grammar/vocab), Speaking & Listening.** This reference covers the first three since they drive workbook content. Use it to pitch material at the right level.

## International & Vietnam mapping

The strands above follow **US Common Core ELA (Grades 3–5)**. Map by age for other systems — the bands are the same:

| Requested system | Equivalent | Subject name |
|---|---|---|
| **Vietnam — Lớp 3 / 4 / 5** (Chương trình GDPT 2018) | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | Môn **Tiếng Việt** |
| UK — Year 4 / 5 / 6 | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | English (NC of England) |
| IB PYP — ages 8–11 | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | Language (Phase 3–4) |

**When the user requests Vietnamese (tiếng Việt):**
- The ELA strands map onto **Tiếng Việt**: Reading → **Đọc hiểu**; Writing → **Tập làm văn**; Language/grammar → **Luyện từ và câu**; plus Vietnam-specific strands **Chính tả** (spelling) and **Tập viết** (handwriting).
- Use Vietnamese **mentor texts** — truyện cổ tích (folk tales), fables, short informational passages about Vietnam (animals, history, geography) — at the target grade's reading level.
- Follow **GDPT 2018** naming and expectations. If a skill sits at a different Lớp in the Vietnamese curriculum, defer to the user's framework and note it.

## Grade 3 (ages 8–9)

**Reading**
- Decode multisyllabic words; know common prefixes (un-, re-, pre-, dis-) and suffixes (-ful, -less, -ly, -er)
- Determine meaning of unknown words using context and affixes
- **Reading literature:** recount stories; determine central message/lesson; describe characters (traits, motivations, feelings)
- **Reading informational:** main idea and key details; use text features (headings, captions, glossary)
- Point of view (who is telling the story); distinguish own point of view from the narrator's

**Writing**
- Opinion pieces: state opinion, give reasons, concluding statement
- Informative pieces: introduce a topic, facts/definitions, linking words, closing
- Narratives: establish situation, sequence of events, dialogue, sense of closure
- Introduce research: gather information from sources; take brief notes

**Language (grammar & vocabulary)**
- Nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs; abstract nouns (childhood, friendship)
- Regular and irregular plural nouns; subject–verb agreement
- Simple, compound, and complex sentences (introduced)
- Capitalization: titles, proper nouns; commas in addresses and in a series
- Frequently confused words: their/there/they're, to/too/two
- Use glossaries/dictionaries; shades of meaning among related words

## Grade 4 (ages 9–10)

**Reading**
- **Literature:** summarize (not just recount); determine theme; describe in depth a character, setting, or event drawing on details; compare/contrast point of view
- **Informational:** refer to details/examples when explaining text; summarize; explain events/procedures/concepts; integrate information from two texts on the same topic
- First-person vs. third-person narration; structures: chronology, cause/effect, problem/solution

**Writing**
- Opinion: clearly state opinion, group related reasons, supply reasons supported by facts/details, link opinion and reasons with words/phrases, concluding section
- Informative: group related information; develop with facts, definitions, concrete details, quotations; precise language and domain-specific vocabulary
- Narratives: orient the reader, use dialogue and description, use transitional words/phrases, pacing, concrete words, conclusions
- One-page writing (typed or handwritten) appropriate to task and audience
- Research: short research projects; recall relevant info; take notes and categorize

**Language**
- Relative pronouns and adverbs (who, whose, which, that; where, when, why)
- Progressive verb tenses; modal auxiliaries (can, may, must)
- Prepositional phrases; frequently confused words (to/too/two; there/their/they're)
- Fragments and run-ons; correct use of commas and quotation marks in dialogue
- Greek and Latin affixes and roots as clues to meaning; word relationships (synonyms, antonyms, idioms, adages)
- Use context as a clue; consult reference materials for pronunciation/meaning

## Grade 5 (ages 10–11)

**Reading**
- **Literature:** quote accurately when drawing inferences; determine theme including how characters respond; compare/contrast two or more characters/settings/events; explain how a series of chapters/scenes fits together; narrators and point of view
- **Informational:** quote accurately; explain relationships/interactions among individuals/events/ideas/concepts; multiple main ideas; reason/evidence; draw on multiple sources
- Analyze how visual and multimedia elements contribute to meaning, tone, or beauty

**Writing**
- Opinion: introduce topic clearly, state opinion, provide logically ordered reasons supported by facts/details, link ideas, provide concluding section; introduce a claim
- Informative: develop topic with facts, definitions, concrete details, quotations, multimedia; organize with headings, illustrations; precise vocabulary
- Narratives: develop real or imagined experiences; establish context and point of view; use narrative techniques (dialogue, pacing, description); logical sequence; conclusions
- Multi-paragraph writing; strengthen writing by planning, revising, editing, rewriting, or trying a new approach
- Research: conduct short research projects using several sources; summarize/paraphrase; provide a list of sources

**Language**
- Conjunctions, prepositions, interjections; correlative conjunctions (either/or, neither/nor)
- Perfect verb tenses; inappropriate shifts in verb tense
- Verbals — explain function by their form and place (introduced)
- Use underlining, quotation marks, or italics for titles; punctuation to separate items in a series; commas with introductory elements and tag questions
- Expand, combine, and reduce sentences for meaning and style
- Consult reference materials; interpret figurative language (similes, metaphors); relate words to antonyms/synonyms; use grade-appropriate Greek/Latin affixes and roots

## Worked-example norms by grade

- **Grade 3:** show a short sample sentence or short paragraph and annotate it ("This underlined word is an adjective because it describes the noun").
- **Grade 4:** show a 2–4 sentence passage; underline/quote the specific evidence the skill targets. Begin modeling how to find evidence in text.
- **Grade 5:** use a full short paragraph; model quoting text accurately and explaining the inference. Show the difference between "the text says" (evidence) and "so this means" (inference).

## Writing-task scaffolding by grade

When the lesson includes a student writing task, scaffold appropriately:

- **Grade 3:** graphic organizer with 3 boxes (opinion / 2 reasons / closing); students fill in, then write one paragraph.
- **Grade 4:** organizer + modeled paragraph; students write a structured 1-paragraph piece (4–6 sentences) with a linking word.
- **Grade 5:** organizer + model; students write a multi-paragraph response (introduction, body, conclusion) quoting evidence.

## Common pitch errors to avoid

- Treating **"quote accurately from the text"** as a Grade 3 skill — it's emphasized in Grade 5.
- Asking Grade 3 to **determine theme abstractly** — Grade 3 is the *central message/lesson*; theme analysis deepens at Grade 4–5.
- Pushing **perfect verb tenses** before Grade 5.
- Asking Grade 3–4 to write **multi-paragraph essays** — that's a Grade 5 expectation.
- Teaching **figurative language (metaphor)** as a central skill before Grade 5 (it's introduced, but the analytical expectation is Grade 5).
- Confusing **summarize (Grade 4–5)** with **recount (Grade 3)** — summarizing requires judging importance, recounting lists events.

When in doubt about where a topic belongs, stay at the lower grade and add an extension rather than risk pitching too hard.

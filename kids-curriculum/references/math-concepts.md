# Math Concepts — Grades 3–5 Scope & Sequence

Use this to pitch math content at the right level. Each grade lists what's **new/in-focus** that year. Prior grades' content is fair game for spiral review; next-grade content should NOT appear unless explicitly building a bridge.

## International & Vietnam mapping

The scope below is written against **US Common Core (Grades 3–5)**. For other systems the bands line up closely by age, so map directly:

| Requested system | Equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Vietnam — Lớp 3 / 4 / 5** (Chương trình GDPT 2018) | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | Môn **Toán** ≈ Math. Same age band; broadly compatible sequence. |
| UK — Year 4 / 5 / 6 | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | National Curriculum of England |
| IB PYP — ages 8–11 | Grade 3 / 4 / 5 | Phase 3–4 |

**When the user requests Vietnamese (tiếng Việt):**
- Write all student-facing content in Vietnamese (mon **Toán**). Keep the teacher lesson plan in whatever language the user is using.
- Use Vietnamese contexts in word problems — **đồng (VND)** not dollars; local names, foods (phở, bánh mì), and festivals (Tết, Trung Thu) — so problems feel real, not translated.
- Follow **GDPT 2018** naming and conventions. The sequence above is compatible; if a topic sits at a different Lớp in the Vietnamese curriculum, defer to the user's framework and say so.

## Grade 3 (ages 8–9)

**Number & Operations**
- Multiplication: understand as equal groups; memorize facts up to 10×10 (focus on ×2, ×5, ×10, then ×3, ×4, then the rest)
- Division: understand as sharing/equal groups; basic division facts tied to multiplication
- Place value to 10,000; round to nearest 10 or 100
- Add/subtract within 1,000 fluently; strategies for within 10,000
- Fractions: unit fractions (1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6, 1/8) on a number line; equivalent fractions with models; comparing fractions with same numerator or denominator

**Measurement, Data, Geometry**
- Time to the minute; elapsed time
- Liquid volume (liters/mL), mass (grams/kilograms)
- Area as tiling / multiplication; perimeter of polygons
- Scaled picture and bar graphs; line plots with fractional units
- Quadrilaterals: rhombuses, rectangles, squares — attributes and categories

## Grade 4 (ages 9–10)

**Number & Operations**
- Place value to 1,000,000; round to any place
- Multi-digit multiplication (up to 4-digit × 1-digit and 2-digit × 2-digit)
- Long division with up to 4-digit dividends and 1-digit divisors (remainder as a number)
- Fraction equivalence and comparison using multiplication; adding and subtracting fractions with like denominators; multiplying a fraction by a whole number
- Decimal notation for tenths and hundredths; decimal–fraction equivalence; compare decimals

**Measurement, Data, Geometry**
- Unit conversion within one measurement system (m→cm, hr→min)
- Area and perimeter formulas for rectangles; apply to word problems
- Angle measurement; additive angles; using a protractor conceptually
- Line plots with fractional data; reading and interpreting

## Grade 5 (ages 10–11)

**Number & Operations**
- Place value to the thousandths; powers of 10
- Multi-digit multiplication fluent (any number of digits); division of whole numbers (up to 4-digit ÷ 2-digit)
- Add/subtract/multiply fractions with unlike denominators; divide unit fractions by whole numbers and whole numbers by unit fractions
- Decimal operations: add, subtract, multiply, divide to hundredths
- Volume of rectangular prisms as packing and as a formula (V = l × w × h); relate to multiplication and addition

**Expressions, Data, Geometry**
- Write and interpret numerical expressions (order of operations, no exponents yet, though × may appear)
- Coordinate plane first quadrant (introduced, not central) — depending on standard
- Convert like measurement units within a system
- Read and make line plots with fractional units and use them to solve problems
- Classify two-dimensional figures by properties (hierarchy of polygons)

## Worked-example norms by grade

- **Grade 3:** draw the groups/array/picture as part of the worked example. Always show the model.
- **Grade 4:** show the algorithm step-by-step with arrows or labeled steps. Picture optional but helpful for fractions.
- **Grade 5:** show the algorithm; for volume, include a labeled prism. Begin to show *why* (e.g., partial products) when introducing a new method.

## Common pitch errors to avoid

- Putting **long division** in Grade 3 — it's a Grade 4 skill.
- Treating **unlike-denominator fraction operations** as Grade 4 — they're Grade 5. Grade 4 is like denominators only.
- Asking Grade 3 to **multiply fractions** — that's Grade 4 (fraction × whole number) or Grade 5 (fraction × fraction).
- Using **decimals before Grade 4** — decimals are introduced in Grade 4 (tenths/hundredths) and operated on in Grade 5.
- Mixing **volume (Grade 5)** with simple area (Grade 3/4) in one lesson as if they're the same idea.

When in doubt about where a topic belongs, stay at the lower grade and add an extension rather than risk pitching too hard.

## Visual models to default to

For grades 3–4 especially, **don't just describe the model in text — draw it as a simple inline SVG** on the workbook page (see the "Draw it" section in SKILL.md). The table below tells you *which* model to reach for; turn it into an SVG when the concept is visual.

| Concept | Default model | Draw as SVG? |
|---|---|---|
| Fractions | Number line + area/bar model | ✅ bar with shaded parts; marked number line |
| Multiplication | Array + equal-groups drawing | ✅ grid/array of dots or squares |
| Division | Tape diagram + sharing into groups | ✅ tape/bar split into equal groups |
| Place value | Base-ten blocks / place-value chart | ⚠️ optional — a place-value chart is easy |
| Area/perimeter | Grid squares | ✅ rectangle on a unit grid |
| Decimals | Place-value chart + 10×10 grid | ✅ 10×10 grid with squares shaded |
| Word problems | Bar / tape model | ✅ two compared bars (part-part-whole) |

SVG defaults: one accent fill (`#3B82F6` or `#F59E0B`), thin dark strokes, labels in the student's language. When in doubt whether the SVG will be correct, omit it and leave labeled work space — a wrong diagram teaches the wrong thing.

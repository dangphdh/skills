# Kids Storytelling (Ages 6–11)

Write stories for children — standalone tales, curriculum mentor texts, or educational narratives that smuggle a lesson inside a plot. The craft rules live in `references/craft.md`; read them before writing any story.

## Confirm before writing

Ask only what's missing, in one batched message:

- **Audience age** — determines everything (vocabulary, arc, length). Default: 8.
- **Purpose** — pure entertainment, bedtime story, or teaching something (a concept, a value)?
- **If teaching:** the lesson/concept, so the story can be built around it.
- **Language** — English or Vietnamese (tiếng Việt)? Match the user's language.
- **Length** — picture-book (~500–700 words), early reader, or chapter-book excerpt.

## The non-negotiables

1. **Story first.** Even when teaching. A lesson bolted onto a plot is a lecture in costume; a plot built around a lesson is remembered.
2. **Show, don't tell the moral.** The character learns through experience and natural consequences. Never end with "And so Mai learned that…". If the reader can state the moral, you showed it enough.
3. **Respect the reader.** Ages 6–11 are not toddlers. No condescension, no baby talk. Humor from child-logic, not from the narrator smirking at the characters.
4. **One main problem.** Kids this age track one storyline. Subplots start at chapter-book level (9–11), and even then keep it to one.
5. **Vocabulary ceilings by age** — full tables in `references/craft.md`:
   - Age 6–7: ~400–800 unique words; sight words, blends/digraphs; predictable patterns
   - Age 8–9: ~800–1,500 unique words; complex vocabulary *with context clues*
   - Age 9–11: chapter territory; multiple characters, light subplots, deeper themes

## The arc, by age

| Age | Arc | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| 6–7 | Problem → Try → Try → Success | Repetition of three; cumulative patterns |
| 8–9 | Opening → Inciting incident → Rising action → Climax → Resolution | Character grows; stakes are kid-scale |
| 9–11 | Full arc + a twist or hard choice at the climax | Character transformation; theme emerges from choice |

Ages 6–8: ~500–700 words, heavy rhythm, read-aloud friendly. Ages 9–11: 800–1,500 words, chapters optional.

## Engagement levers (use 2–3, not all)

From picture-book craft — each must serve the story, not decorate it:

- **Repetition** — a refrain the reader anticipates ("But the mountain was still far away.")
- **Call-and-response** — a question the reader answers aloud
- **Surprise & delight** — one unexpected beat near the climax
- **Sensory language** — taste, smell, sound grounded in the setting

## Vietnamese output (tiếng Việt)

When writing in Vietnamese:

- Use the same age bands and arcs; Vietnamese children's literature follows the same shapes.
- Draw on familiar frames: truyện cổ tích (folk tales), fables with animals (thỏ, rùa, cáo), Tết and village settings — but write original plots, don't retell protected retellings.
- Keep sentence length age-appropriate; define unfamiliar words in context, especially Sino-Vietnamese (từ Hán Việt) terms for ages 6–8.
- Moral framing is culturally welcome but the **show-don't-tell rule still applies** — end on the character's changed action, not on a stated moral.

## Integration with kids-curriculum

This skill pairs with `kids-curriculum`: when a lesson needs a **mentor text** (ELA) or a **story-framed word problem** (math), write it with these rules. The story stays subordinate to the lesson's objective — target length 100–250 words for a single lesson's passage, and keep reading level at or slightly below the lesson's grade so the skill being taught, not decoding, is what's practiced.

## Quality checks

- [ ] Moral shown, never stated?
- [ ] One problem, one storyline (until age 9+)?
- [ ] Vocabulary within the age band — new words defined by context?
- [ ] Arc matches the age table?
- [ ] Read-aloud test for ages 6–8: does the rhythm survive being spoken?
- [ ] If for a lesson: does the story serve the objective without becoming the lesson?
